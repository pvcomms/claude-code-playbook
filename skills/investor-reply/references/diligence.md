# Counter-Diligence: Questions Param Should Have Answered

Fundraising is two-way diligence. Investors vet founders; founders must vet investors. For a solo founder raising pre-seed, the wrong investor costs more than a bad hire — they sit on the cap table forever.

Pick **4–8** of these per diligence sheet. Weight by scenario. Don't dump all of them.

## Fund structure / check

1. What's the typical first check size at pre-seed for this fund?
2. Do they lead or follow? At pre-seed specifically.
3. What ownership do they target at entry?
4. How much follow-on reserve per company? (Funds with no reserves can't support a down round.)
5. What's the fund's AUM and current vintage? (Older funds nearing end-of-life push for exits that don't match founder timelines.)
6. Are they fund I, II, III? Fund I GPs are often more flexible but less experienced at managing founder stress.
7. Is this the partner's personal allocation or the main fund?

## Stage fit for Keep specifically

8. Have they invested in pre-revenue consumer products where the wedge is content, not paid acquisition?
9. Do they have a position on prediction markets / real-money consumer products / regulated consumer?
10. How do they think about products that need offshore licensing (Gibraltar) to unlock the real-money TAM?
11. Have they done biometric / wearables plays? Which ones? What did they learn?
12. Have they funded solo founders? What's the partner's stated position on solo vs. cofounder teams?

## Time and conviction

13. What's the typical time from first call to term sheet at this fund?
14. How many partner meetings between first call and decision?
15. How many companies did they pass on in the last 12 months that they now wish they'd done? (Reveals humility and taste.)
16. What's the most unusual founder profile they've backed recently?
17. What conviction-level check is this for them on first meeting — "interesting, let's see" or "we actually might write"? (Ask directly. Most will dodge. The ones who answer are the ones to work with.)

## Value-add that matters for Param specifically

18. Bay Area intros — can they introduce Param to 5-10 specific founders in the consumer health / prediction markets / biometrics space within a month?
19. Downstream investor intros at seed and Series A — which funds do they overlap with most?
20. LP base — any LPs who would be helpful as customers / design partners / operators?
21. If Param relocates to the Bay via an incubator, does this investor help with the logistics (visa, housing, intros)?
22. Board involvement — how many boards does the partner sit on currently? (>8 = they're too busy to help.)
23. What's their content distribution? Do they amplify portfolio companies on their own channels, or is the founder fully on their own for distribution?

## Reference checks to request

24. Two founders they backed who are doing well. (Easy.)
25. **Two founders they passed on or who failed** — and how the investor behaved. This is the real signal. Investors who only give you winners are hiding something.
26. One founder in their portfolio from Asia / non-US / non-standard background. (Tests whether they actually back outsiders or just tokenize.)
27. The most recent founder they had a real disagreement with. What was the disagreement, how did it resolve?

## Red-flag questions (ask carefully)

28. Have they ever fired a founder they backed? What was the process?
29. Have they ever led a pay-to-play or down round where early investors got cleaned up?
30. What's their stance on secondaries at seed / Series A? (Some funds aggressively push founders to sell. Some refuse.)
31. Do they require information rights, board observer, or pro-rata at pre-seed? If yes, why at this stage?
32. How do they behave when a company misses plan two quarters in a row?

## Keep-specific regulatory / risk

33. How do they think about the Gibraltar licensing path vs. alternative jurisdictions (Curacao, Isle of Man, offshore Malta)?
34. Comfort with products that sit in a gray zone early (play-money) and transition to regulated later?
35. Position on prediction markets in the US post-Kalshi rulings — do they believe the regulatory ceiling has been unlocked, or still betting against?
36. Any conflict with existing portfolio companies (Kalshi, Polymarket, Manifold, Beeminder, StickK, etc.)?

## Cultural / founder fit

37. Is the partner in Param's timezone most of the time, or does working together mean Param flips his sleep schedule for quarterly board calls?
38. How do they handle founder mental health / solo founder isolation? (Or do they?)
39. Are they op-ed / content investors (Bilal at Box Group, Hunter Walk, Elad Gil style) or purely pattern-matching? Content investors tend to be better for a content-first founder like Param.
40. Are they comfortable with founders who publish publicly, including posts that may be controversial or critical of VC culture?

## The one question to always ask

**"What would make you say no after this call?"** This flips the diligence. Most VCs will deflect. The ones who give a specific answer — "if you can't articulate the regulatory path" or "if the Whoop API dependency is actually fragile" — are the ones worth working with because they're showing you their actual decision criteria.

## Scenario-based picks

- **Cold associate:** Q1, Q7, Q14, Q17 — determine whether they can actually make a decision.
- **Partner direct:** Q2, Q8-12, Q17, Q25, Q40 — the real ones, since the partner can answer.
- **Warm intro:** Q8, Q18-20, Q25, The One Question — they're pre-qualified on the basics, go deeper.
- **Scout:** Q1, Q7, Q13, Q40 — scouts have limited answers to the deeper ones.
- **Accelerator:** different set — cohort timing, equity %, alumni outcomes, in-person requirement, visa support, demo day investor quality, office space quality. Include Q18 and Q40.
- **Angel:** Q8 (in their area), Q18, Q21. Skip fund-structure questions.
- **Family office:** Q1-7 heavily — structure questions matter most because these entities are opaque.
