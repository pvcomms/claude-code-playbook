# Inbound VC Scenarios

Classify every inbound into one of these before drafting. The reply changes materially by scenario.

## 1. Cold associate outreach

**Tells:** associate/analyst title, no prior interaction, generic opener ("came across your work", "really impressed by what you're building at Keep"), calendar link attached, no specific reference to any Keep essay or product detail.

**Reality:** this is sourcing work. Associates are paid to fill partner pipelines. Meeting an associate at a multi-stage fund rarely converts to a term sheet — it converts to "partner will circle back after the associate presents at Monday meeting." For a solo founder, that's 3 meetings of free diligence for a <5% conversion.

**Posture:** polite deferral or redirect-to-partner. Do not take the call just because they asked.

**Reply shape (4 sentences):**

- Acknowledge the note.
- One specific fact about where Keep actually is (e.g. "content-first right now, real money 6-18 months out pending Gibraltar license").
- Counter-ask: "if the partner you source for actively backs consumer prediction markets / biometrics at pre-seed, happy to chat directly. Otherwise let's reconnect when there's something to show."
- Sign off.

## 2. Warm intro from a mutual

**Tells:** forwarded thread, "[Mutual] said you'd be the right person to talk to", or a mutual CC'd.

**Reality:** warm intros deserve warm replies. The mutual staked reputation. Reply quickly (same day), reply fully, propose a specific time window.

**Posture:** take the call unless there's an obvious mismatch.

**Reply shape (5-7 sentences):**

- Thank the mutual by name once (not effusively).
- Engage with whatever the investor actually said about Keep — no boilerplate.
- Link the landing page + one essay that frames the thesis.
- Propose two specific time slots in Param's timezone (IST) AND one that works for SF/NYC.
- Offer async alternative if they prefer: "happy to send a voice memo walkthrough instead if that's easier".
- Sign off.

## 3. Partner / GP direct

**Tells:** Partner title, personal email (not calendly bot), references a specific essay or product detail, sometimes a contrarian take of their own, often shorter than an associate email.

**Reality:** this is the meeting worth taking. Partners write checks. Partners also read the reply more carefully than associates do — so voice and specificity matter more here.

**Posture:** take the call. But still do counter-diligence.

**Reply shape (5-6 sentences):**

- Acknowledge the specific thing they referenced.
- Add one piece of context or a contrarian nuance that rewards them for having read carefully.
- Say yes to the meeting. Propose times.
- One counter-ask disguised as a comment: "curious which of your portfolio companies you think is closest in shape — helps me calibrate."
- Sign off.

## 4. Scout

**Tells:** mentions they're a scout for [big fund], often a founder or operator themselves, check sizes small ($25-$250k), decision speed fast.

**Reality:** scouts can move fast and are often better signal than associates. But scout checks are often awkward on the cap table — they rarely lead and sometimes come with weird fund-of-fund dynamics.

**Posture:** take the call if the scout themselves is interesting (operator, founder, specific domain). Otherwise defer.

**Reply shape (4-5 sentences):**

- Quick acknowledge.
- Ask them directly: "what's the typical scout check size for you and what decision timeline should I expect?" — reveals whether they actually have allocation or are fishing.
- If they're operator-scouts, add one specific question about their operating experience.
- Propose a 20-min call, not 30.
- Sign off.

## 5. Accelerator / incubator / program

**Tells:** program name (YC, Techstars, On Deck, South Park Commons, Neo, AI Grant, etc.), application link, cohort dates, equity terms sometimes mentioned upfront.

**Reality:** directly relevant — Param is actively planning a Bay Area incubator move in the next 3-6 months (memory: project_bay_area_incubator.md). Reply should signal interest without committing.

**Posture:** engage substantively. Ask about the specific cohort timing, equity terms, physical presence requirements, and alumni outcomes in consumer / health / prediction markets.

**Reply shape (5-7 sentences):**

- Acknowledge.
- State the truth: "actively exploring relocating to the Bay for exactly this kind of setup in the next 3-6 months."
- Ask 2-3 specific questions: cohort timing, % equity taken, in-person requirement, alumni founders building in consumer health / prediction markets.
- Propose a call.
- Sign off.

## 6. Angel (individual)

**Tells:** no fund name, personal email, often a founder/operator, check sizes $10k-$100k, sometimes mentions a specific check history.

**Reality:** angels with domain (biometrics, prediction markets, consumer health, regulated products) are gold. Generic angels collecting logos are not.

**Posture:** take the call if domain-relevant. Otherwise defer and add to list for a rolling angel round later.

**Reply shape (4-5 sentences):**

- Ack.
- Ask about their angel history specifically in the relevant vertical.
- If the fit is there, propose a call. If not, offer to keep them posted when Param opens a round.
- Sign off.

## 7. Family office / private capital

**Tells:** "family office", "private investment vehicle", "investing personal capital for [principal]", often generic, often from a wealth-management-adjacent email domain.

**Reality:** usually slower decision cycles, looser thesis, sometimes looking for access rather than alpha. Occasionally extraordinary — sometimes garbage.

**Posture:** ask pointed questions before committing time. "What's the typical check size and hold period? What stage do you typically enter at?"

## 8. Generic "let's chat" with no thesis

**Tells:** 2-3 sentences, no specifics, calendar link, no reference to Keep at all.

**Reality:** volume outreach. Ignore or polite-decline.

**Posture:** polite decline or no reply at all. If Param wants to keep the option open, a one-line "not raising right now, will reach out when I am" is fine.

## 9. Follow-up on a prior interaction

**Tells:** references a prior email, meeting, DM, or conference.

**Reality:** respect the continuity. Read the prior thread (or ask Param for the gist) before drafting.

**Posture:** depends entirely on how the prior interaction went. If Param paused it, the new reply should reference why.

## Red flags that downgrade any scenario

- Misspelled "Param" or "Keep" or "Vaswani"
- Wrong company name ("love what you're doing at Lifebet" — Keep, not Lifebet; pivoted Apr 17)
- Template tells: "I saw you're building in the [X] space and thought we should connect"
- Calendar link with no proposed times
- "Circle back", "touch base", "let's explore synergies"
- Asking for the deck in the first email
- Addressing a cofounder by name (Param is solo)
- Claiming to have read an essay but mischaracterizing it
