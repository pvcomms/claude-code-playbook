# Email Voice (Founder → Investor)

Param's email voice is not his essay voice. Essays are literary, emails are utilitarian. Same person, different register.

## Core rules

- **Lowercase sign-off.** Always `Param` on its own line. No "Best,", "Cheers,", "Thanks,", "Regards,". No signature block. No pronouns line.
- **No em-dashes in email.** Param uses em-dashes heavily in essays. In email they read as anxious over-explaining. Use periods or semicolons instead.
- **No exclamation marks. Ever.** Not in greetings, not in sign-offs, not in replies.
- **No "Happy to..." as opener.** It's a servile tell. Replace with the actual action: "Can do a call Thursday."
- **No "Just wanted to..."** Passive apology. Delete the "just".
- **No "quick question" / "quick chat" / "quick call"** — saying "quick" never makes it quick, and it telegraphs that Param's time is cheap.
- **Contractions on.** "I'm", "we're", "can't", "don't". Not "I am", "we are", "cannot".
- **No name in greeting on replies.** "Hey [Name]," at the start of an in-thread reply is redundant. Just start. Greeting allowed on the first message in a brand-new thread (rare for replies).
- **First word is the real word.** Don't burn line one on pleasantries. "Thanks for the note" is acceptable once, only if warranted.

## Vocabulary bans

Never use:

- circle back, touch base, sync, jump on a call, hop on
- deep dive, double-click, peel the onion
- space (as in "the prediction markets space"), ecosystem, landscape
- exciting, thrilled, stoked, pumped
- reach out (as a verb — "thanks for reaching out" is the worst opener)
- the founder journey, the startup journey, my journey
- vision, mission, our why
- disrupt, disruptor, disruption
- leverage (as a verb)
- let's explore, let's chat, let's connect, let's see if there's a fit
- mindful, authentic, passionate
- at scale, scalable (unless literal)
- game-changer, needle-mover, no-brainer
- "I'd love to" (replace with "happy to" sparingly, or just commit: "Can do Thursday")

Use instead:

- "let's talk" not "let's connect"
- "we're not there yet" not "too early"
- "not for us" not "not a fit"
- "call Thursday 9am IST / Wed 8:30pm PT" not "happy to find a time that works"
- "the thesis is" not "our vision is"

## Opening lines (first sentence of the reply)

Pick from this bank, vary by scenario:

- "Thanks for the note."
- "Appreciated the read on [specific thing they said]."
- "Good timing."
- "Tell me more about [specific thing in their email]."
- "[Mutual's name] sent a good signal."
- (For cold/generic): just start with the substance. No opener.

Never use:

- "Thanks so much for reaching out!"
- "Really appreciate you taking the time..."
- "What a great email..."
- "Thrilled to hear from someone at [fund]..."

## Specificity test

Before sending, re-read the reply. If you could swap "Keep" for any other startup and "Param" for any other founder and the email still made sense — it's too generic. Rewrite with one detail only Param could have written:

- A specific Whoop/Oura behavior pattern you've observed
- The Gibraltar real-money licensing timeline
- The charity-forfeit mechanic (distinct from pure prediction markets)
- The content-first GTM (Substack / essays feed Keep, not the other way around)
- The Bangalore → Bay Area move reasoning
- A line from a Keep essay the VC referenced

## Length calibration

- **Default: 4-7 sentences.** Shorter than VCs expect from founders. This is intentional.
- **Cold associate / generic:** 3-4 sentences. Under 60 words.
- **Warm intro / partner:** 5-7 sentences. 90-130 words.
- **Accelerator / incubator:** 6-8 sentences. 130-180 words.
- **Decline:** 2 sentences. "Not raising right now. Will be in touch if that changes. Param."

## Response speed signals

- **Warm intro from mutual:** reply same day.
- **Partner direct:** reply within 24h.
- **Associate cold:** reply 2-4 days out is fine. Same-hour replies signal availability.
- **Generic blast:** no reply or one-week reply is acceptable.

If Param asks for the draft immediately after receiving the email, note in the internal section: "recommend waiting [X hours/days] before sending."

## Counter-ask pattern

Every reply except declines should include something Param asks of them — never just passive availability. Examples:

- "Which of your portfolio companies do you think is closest in shape?"
- "What does a second meeting look like if this one goes well?"
- "What's the typical check size you'd lead at pre-seed?"
- "Which of my essays did you read?"
- "Are you looking at consumer health / prediction markets as a wedge or just founder-by-founder?"

The counter-ask is what separates a founder being diligenced from a founder doing their own diligence.

## The Keep context to weave in (pick ONE per reply, not all)

- Content-first GTM: Substack + essays are the wedge, not paid acquisition
- Whoop/Oura settlement layer as the oracle differentiator
- Play-money first (fast), Gibraltar real-money license 6-18 months out (slow)
- Charity-forfeit stakes — this is NOT a prediction market on sports; it's commitment markets
- Beeminder is the closest comp, but Keep is peer-settled not self-reported
- Solo founder, Bangalore, actively planning Bay Area move 3-6 months out

## Never-ever

- Never attach a deck in the first reply.
- Never quote a revenue, burn, or round-size number.
- Never name other investors in the process in first contact.
- Never say "fundraising" if Param isn't actively raising (he usually isn't — content-first).
- Never say "I'll think about it and get back to you" — decide in the email.
